# TAPPaaS — 5-Minute Promotional Video Script

**Format:** Lars on camera, 3 supporting slides
**Target length:** ~5:00 (≈685 spoken words at a calm 145 wpm)
**Slides:** `tappaas-promo-video-slides.pptx`

---

## [0:00 – 0:40] Opening — camera only, no slide

> Let me ask you a simple question: who is actually in control of your organisation's digital life?
>
> Your data — where it lives, who can read it, whose law applies to it. Your costs — the cloud bill that only ever goes up. And your access — whether you could still reach your own systems if a provider, a policy, or a geopolitical decision changed tomorrow.
>
> For most organisations, the honest answer is: someone else. We've traded control for convenience. And in a world that is getting less stable, not more, that trade is starting to look expensive.
>
> Hello — my name is Lars Rossen. I'm the co-founder of the TAPPaaS project, and of the Sovereign Computing initiative. Let me show you why we need to pivot how we do cloud.

*(≈125 words. Direct to camera, conversational. The three words to land: **data, cost, access**. The intro lands after the hook, so the viewer already cares before hearing who you are.)*

---

## [0:40 – 2:00] Slide 1 — Cloud continuum

> The cloud isn't one thing — it's a continuum.
>
> On the left, the public hyperscalers: fast, convenient, and outside your control. As you move right, you gain sovereignty: sovereign controls, EU entities, EU clouds — and at the far end, private cloud. Your hardware, your premises, your jurisdiction. Full control.
>
> Notice the glass wall. The hyperscalers know sovereignty matters — they're all moving right, adding "sovereign" offerings. But they can never cross that wall. As long as your infrastructure is owned and operated under someone else's jurisdiction, your sovereignty is a promise in a contract — not a fact of architecture.
>
> And this isn't only about jurisdiction. It's about resilience. On the right side of the wall, your systems keep running even if the connection to the outside world doesn't. Connected to the internet — but not dependent on it.
>
> So if the right side is where control and resilience live... why isn't everyone there already?

*(≈165 words. The closing question is the bridge to slide 2.)*

---

## [2:00 – 3:30] Slide 2 — The hurdle: what a cloud really is

> Here's the uncomfortable truth: the hyperscalers earned their position. Their real product was never servers — it was **packaging**. They took hundreds of complicated services and wrapped them into something easy, robust, and automated.
>
> Because a real cloud platform comes down to four building blocks. A **site** — where your data lives and your AI runs. **Workloads** — the applications your organisation actually uses: files, collaboration, automation, AI. **People** — identity, sign-on, who has access to what. And **environments** — the security boundaries, the backup, the patching, the guardrails.
>
> If you wanted to self-host all of that yourself, you'd have to become your own cloud provider: integrate dozens of open-source components, keep them secure, keep them patched, keep them backed up. That's the hurdle. That's why organisations that *want* sovereignty still end up on the left side of the glass wall.
>
> TAPPaaS removes that hurdle. It packages the entire thing — all four building blocks, pre-integrated, automated, and open source. The innovation isn't inventing new components. It's integrating and governing proven ones — so self-hosting feels like the cloud.

*(≈195 words. The key message: hyperscalers packaged complexity; TAPPaaS packages the same convenience on your side of the wall.)*

---

## [3:30 – 4:30] Slide 3 — Vision of TAPPaaS

> TAPPaaS stands for **Trusted, Automated, Private Platform as a Service** — self-hosted.
>
> Trusted: security by design, fully open source, so there's nothing you can't inspect and no vendor that can lock you in. Automated: installation, patching, and backup are handled for you — matching the cloud experience, not fighting it. Private: it runs on your own inexpensive hardware, under your roof and your law — which also makes it remarkably cost-effective.
>
> And resilient by design: backed up, and ready to run off-grid when it has to.
>
> This isn't a slide-deck vision. TAPPaaS is running today — serving files, running AI models locally, automating workflows — and the first companies are already adopting it.

*(≈120 words.)*

---

## [4:30 – 5:00] Close — camera only, no slide

> Sovereignty and resilience are not nostalgia, and they're not anti-cloud. They're simply about being able to answer that first question — *who is in control?* — with: **we are**.
>
> TAPPaaS is open source, and it's ready for you to try. Go to **tappaas.org** — run it, join the community, or talk to us about bringing your organisation to the right side of the glass wall.
>
> Your data. Your costs. Your access. Your platform.

*(≈80 words. Ends by echoing the opening triad + CTA.)*

---

## Production notes

- **Total spoken:** ≈685 words → ~4:45 at 145 wpm, leaving ~15s of breathing room for pauses and slide transitions.
- **Slide screen time:** slide 1 ≈ 80s, slide 2 ≈ 90s, slide 3 ≈ 60s. Cut back to camera for open and close so the video starts and ends with you, not a slide.
- **One-line teleprompter anchors:** (1) Who is in control — data, cost, access. (2) The glass wall — sovereignty is architecture, not a contract. (3) Hyperscalers packaged complexity; TAPPaaS packages it on your side of the wall. (4) Trusted, Automated, Private — and running today. (5) tappaas.org.
- A named early adopter (e.g. Qualiware) can back the "first companies are already adopting it" line if you want a name on screen — check what's public before using it.
- If you run long in rehearsal, the "Compliant" detail in the slide-3 narration is the easiest thing to drop.
