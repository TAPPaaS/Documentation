    asset-manager¶

    The site's physical assets and where they are (ADR-040 D7–D9). asset-manager owns config/positions.json: the rooms and the location each is in, the racks, where each device is placed, the assets — every physical box with its ports, its life from ordered to disposed, and the role it plays — and the few cables nothing can see. It changes no device and no device record: a placement names a device as network-manager topology names it — a node, a switch, an access point, a machine, or a device nothing manages — and only says where that device stands.

    An asset is the thing; its role is what it is for the time being: the topology's name for the node, switch or access point it is allocated to, or for the device nothing manages that it is (the ISP's modem, a patch panel). A placement and a cable end name the role while there is one, and the asset's own name when there is none, so a box keeps its rack, its ports and its cables when its role ends and can take another (asset release, asset allocate).

    assets{} is interim: ADR-040 step 3 moves the assets, with the rooms, racks, placements and cables, into the CMDB that cmdb-controller keeps. Its shape is the CMDB's asset and port classes' attributes, so that migration is a copy. Until then positions.json stays the record and asset-manager reports all of it to the CMDB (below).

    A location is the site's (site-manager location list): a room is in one, a rack is in a room, a device is placed in a rack or loose in a room. A device with no placement is somewhere at its location; the console lists it under that location.

    position-manager (ADR-036) and rack-manager before it are its old names: both answer the same verbs for one release (ADR-040 D7, ADR-035 P2). racks.json was its file's old name; migration 0020 renamed it.

    The console's Site › Physical tab draws it by location, room and rack, with faceplates and the links the switches and LLDP see drawn port to port, through network-manager topology, which carries the positions resolved against the site.

    The record¶

    config/positions.json (schema: schemas/positions-fields.json):

    { "from": { "device": "patch1", "port": "1", "side": "rear" }, "to": { "device": "tappaas2", "port": "enp97s0" }, "kind": "patch", "origin": "seen" }

    • rooms are where racks stand and where a device can stand loose. A room's location names one of the site's locations; unset, it is the primary location.
    • A rack's room names a room record; text that names no room yet is kept, and validate notes it.
    • A placement is in a rack — rack and u, the lowest unit a device takes as the rack numbers its units, height the units it takes, width full, half or third with a slot (its column, 0 at the left), face front or rear — or loose in a room: room, and no unit or size (its plate gives its size) — or, a disk's, in another asset (by its own name, never a disk) at its bay (the slot label, else the disk's by-id name). A disk is placed only so, and nothing else is. A device has at most one placement.
    • faceplate is a catalog key; unset, the view draws the catalog's suggestion for the device's model, else a generic plate.
    • assets are the physical things, keyed by name — a tag per kind (srv-NN, sw-NN, ap-NN, dev-NN, dsk-NN: the next free one when asset add or disk add is given none) or the operator's own. Each has a kind (server, switch, access-point, modem, ont, ups, patch-panel, pdu, other, disk), its records — vendor, model (a catalog key where the catalog has one: its ports then come from the plate), serial (unique), assetTag, procurement (supplier, orderRef, purchaseDate, warrantyUntil, ownership owned|rented, notes; no prices) and, once gone, disposal (how sold|recycled| returned|lost, when, wiped) — its ports (below), the location it is at (unset: the primary), its stage and its role.
    • An asset's stage is ordered, stock, placed or disposed as recorded; operational is derived from a role and placed from a placement, and the verbs keep the recorded one in step. faulty is a flag beside the stage, with a faultNote: a node with a failed fan is operational and faulty; a server away for repair is in stock and faulty. An ordered or a disposed asset has no placement and no role; a disposed one's record stays.
    • An asset's role is the name the topology knows it by: a node or a machine (a server's), a switch (a switch's), an access point (an AP's), or — for any other kind — a device nothing manages, which the asset then is to the topology (its role usually its own name). Every asset that is here (not ordered, not disposed) is a device of the topology by its role's name, else its own: it can be placed, it is a cable end, the console draws it.
    • A disk is an asset of its own: the part of a site that fails most, moves between machines and is wiped before it leaves. Its serial is what knows it — unique across the assets, compared without case, spaces and dashes, as tools spell it differently; a by-id name changes with the controller and slot and is only its displayName. Its disk record holds its kind (nvme, ssd, hdd, usb), bus, wwn, size (bytes) and firmware. It has no ports and plays no role; it is no device of the topology. Placed in an asset it is placed; unplaced it is in stock; place --in another asset is the move. An asset that holds disks is neither removed nor disposed of until they are unplaced or moved: whether each was wiped is the disk's disposal.
    • An asset's ports belong to it (ADR-040 D9). A port's kind names its connector — rj45, sfp, wifi, usb-a, usb-c — and its speed is its maximum (rj45 1g, 2.5g, 10g; sfp 1g, 10g, 25g; usb-* usb2, usb3); a wifi port has a standard (wifi5, wifi6, wifi7) and bands (2.4, 5, 6) instead, an sfp cage a module (dac, sr, lr, rj45). role (lan, wan, bmc) says what the node uses it for — a BMC is an rj45 port with role bmc, with its own mac; power, video and serial are no ports. A MAC is unique across every port. A patch panel's port may have a side, front or rear, when it has only one. The ports of an asset's model's plate are its ports; its own entries are merged over them by name (a module in a cage, a MAC), and its own the plate lacks follow.
    • cables are only what nothing can see: the ISP's modem, a device recorded from a switch port, a patch panel, a PDU. A cable the switches or LLDP see comes from the topology and is never recorded. A device only a cable names is still placed by that cable, with its faceplate, and validate says how to record it (asset add). A cable's linkSpeed is the speed seen on it — the connection's, where a port's speed is its maximum: a 10G cage holding a 1G module is speed: 10g with a 1g link.
    • A patch panel's port has a side: its front (the default) and its rear each take one cable. A link split at a panel port (cable split) is two recorded segments marked with their origin — the link the topology sees, or a recorded cable — the device port to the panel port's front, the panel port's rear to the far end. The topology still carries one link between the two devices, with its segments and the panel port it runs through; the Physical view draws the two wires.
    • A passive plate nothing records (a PDU) can still be placed under a name of its own with a passive faceplate.

    Verbs¶

    Verb Does
    validate [--topology <file>] [--json] The whole document: every asset's kind, stage, ports (a speed its connector has, a standard and bands only on wifi, a module only in a cage) and records (serials unique, MACs unique across every port, dates as YYYY-MM-DD), no ordered or disposed asset placed or in a role, a disposed one with its disposal, each role one asset's and one the topology knows (a server's a node or machine, a switch's a switch, an AP's an AP); every placement names a device the topology knows or one recorded here, no two overlap in a rack, each fits its rack, a loose device's room exists, every faceplate key is in the catalog, every cable end names a port the topology, a record or the catalog has, a panel port carries one cable a side; every location a room or a device names is the site's, and a device stands in a room at its own location. It notes a rack's room that is not recorded, a device only a cable names, and a device at a location that has no room (somewhere there, unplaced).
    room list\|show [<room>] [--json] The rooms, their location, their racks and what stands loose in them.
    room add <room> [--location <location>] [--display-name] [--description] A new room, at the primary location unless told.
    room modify <room> --set <field>=<value>... --unset <field>... Its display name, description or location.
    room delete <room> An empty room (no rack names it, nothing stands loose in it).
    asset list [--stage <stage>] [--kind <kind>] [--json] Every asset: its kind, stage (operational and placed derived), role, ports and where it is, the faulty ones marked.
    asset show <asset> [--json] One asset, by its name or the role it plays: its records, ports (its plate's merged with its own), placement, procurement, disposal.
    asset add [<asset>] --kind <kind> [--vendor] [--model <vendor/model>] [--serial] [--asset-tag] [--stage ordered\|stock] [--ports <n> \| --port <name>[:<kind>[:<speed>]]...] [--location <location>] [--display-name] [--description] [--supplier] [--order-ref] [--purchase-date] [--warranty-until] [--ownership owned\|rented] [--notes] A physical thing, bought (--stage ordered) or here (in stock), under its name or the next free tag of its kind. Not a name the topology has: that is a role, which the asset is allocated to.
    asset record <switch>:<port>\|<mac> [--name <asset>] --kind <kind> [--port <name>] [--model] [--location] [--display-name] [--description] A device a switch port sees and nobody recorded (network-manager topology lists it, source: seen) becomes an asset playing itself: its MAC on its port (so it is not seen twice), at the switch's location unless told, shown by what LLDP or the lease calls it, and the port's link recorded as its cable to its port (what LLDP says, else its model's first, else 1).
    asset modify <asset> --set <field>=<value>... --unset <field>... Its kind, vendor, model, serial, assetTag, display name, description, location, ports (a count or a list, rj45 at 1g), mac (its first port's), faultNote, procurement.<field>.
    asset receive <asset> What was ordered has come: in stock.
    asset allocate <asset> --role <name> It takes a role: a node or machine (a server), a switch, an access point the topology knows — or, for another kind, a device nothing manages. Its placement and recorded cables move to the role's name.
    asset release <asset> The role ends: its placement and cables stay with the box, under its own name; it is placed or in stock again.
    asset fault <asset> --note <text> / asset repaired <asset> The faulty flag beside its stage, with what is wrong; cleared.
    asset dispose <asset> --how sold\|recycled\|returned\|lost [--wiped] [--when <date>] [--with-disks \| --disks-to-stock] It leaves the site; refused while it has a role, a placement or a recorded cable (three acts). The record stays, read-only. One that holds disks needs an answer for them: --with-disks takes each disk out and disposes of it the same way (--how, --when, wiped as --wiped says), then the asset; --disks-to-stock takes them out into stock first.
    asset remove <asset> [--with-disks \| --disks-to-stock] A record made by mistake, or a machine that leaves the site: unplaced, no recorded cable, in no role but its own. A disposed asset's record stays. One that holds disks is refused unless told what becomes of them: --with-disks removes each disk's recorded cables, placement and record, then the asset's (the machine leaves with its disks); --disks-to-stock takes them out first (they stay recorded, in stock, for another asset).
    port add <asset> <port> --kind rj45\|sfp\|wifi\|usb-a\|usb-c [--speed] [--standard] [--bands <list>] [--module] [--role lan\|wan\|bmc] [--mac] [--side front\|rear] A port of its own: its connector and what goes with it (rj45 is 1g unless told).
    port modify <asset> <port> --set <field>=<value>... --unset <field>... A port's kind, speed, standard, bands, module, role, mac or side; a port of its model's plate takes an entry of its own, merged over it.
    port remove <asset> <port> A port of its own no recorded cable uses; one over its plate's goes back to the plate's.
    disk list [<asset>] [--json] The disks, each in the asset and bay it is in, or in stock.
    disk add <asset> <name> --serial <s> [--model] [--vendor] [--wwn] [--size <size>] [--kind nvme\|ssd\|hdd\|usb] [--bus] [--firmware] [--bay] [--description] A disk in an asset: the next dsk-NN, <name> (its by-id name) shown, placed in that asset at --bay (default: its name). A serial an asset records already is refused, naming that asset. --size is bytes or 960G, 4TB (1000s), 4TiB (1024s).
    disk modify <disk> --set <field>=<value>... --unset <field>... Its displayName, description, vendor, model, serial (never unset), assetTag, wwn, size, kind, bus, firmware, bay.
    disk remove <disk> A record made by mistake, with its placement. A disposed disk's record stays.
    disk adopt [<asset>] Every disk the CMDB's server CIs list (what site-manager report --discover found) that no asset records yet, by serial, recorded in that server; the recorded ones and those without a serial are skipped, each said. Once, for the servers there are; node add records a new machine's disks itself.
    rack list\|show [<rack>] [--json] The racks; one rack with what is placed in it.
    rack add <rack> [--units 42] [--width 19in\|10in] [--numbering bottom-up\|top-down] [--display-name] [--room] [--description] A new rack.
    rack modify <rack> --set <field>=<value>... --unset <field>... Any field but its name; --unset clears a text.
    rack delete <rack> An empty rack.
    place list [--rack <rack>]\|show <device> [--json] What is placed where.
    place <device> (--rack <rack> --u <n> \| --room <room>) [--height] [--width] [--slot] [--face] [--faceplate] [--label] [--description] A device into a rack, or loose into a room. An asset's plate is its model's, or one drawn from its ports.
    place <disk> --in <asset> [--bay <bay>] A disk into an asset's bay: out of stock, or out of the asset it was in (the move). The bay is the one given, else the one it had there, else its by-id name.
    place modify <device> --set <field>=<value>... --unset <field>... A move (rack and u, slot, face, or room — out of the rack, loose in it) or its plate and label; --unset faceplate goes back to the suggestion.
    unplace <device> The device leaves its rack or room, a disk its asset. A passive plate's recorded cables go with it; an asset keeps its own (and is in stock again).
    cable list [--json] The recorded cables.
    cable add --from <device:port> --to <device:port> [--kind patch\|uplink\|power] [--label] [--from-faceplate\|--to-faceplate <key>] [--from-side\|--to-side front\|rear] A cable nothing can see; a side on a patch panel's port.
    cable remove <device:port> [--side front\|rear] The recorded cable on that port (on a panel, that side's).
    cable split <device:port> --via <panel:port> The link on that port — seen or recorded — through a patch panel port: two recorded segments, the port to the panel port's front, its rear to the far end. The panel port must be free on both sides.
    cable join <device:port> Undo a split, given the panel port or a device end of a segment: the link is the topology's again, or one recorded cable when it was one.
    faceplates list [--json] The catalog: the site's own plates first, marked site, then the shipped ones.
    faceplates show <vendor/model> [--device <name>] [--file] [--json] One plate as text, one line per port row; --device fills a generic plate from that device's ports, with their live state; --file prints it as a catalog file.
    faceplate add <vendor/model> --units <n> --ports <spec> [--width full\|half\|third] [--kind] [--passive] [--match <model>]... [--vendor] [--display-name] [--description] A plate the site adds for a model the catalog lacks (see The site's own plates).
    faceplate modify <vendor/model> --set <field>=<value>... --unset <field>... A site plate's units, width, ports, kind, passive, match, vendor, displayName or description.
    faceplate delete <vendor/model> A site plate nothing places or records; one that replaced a shipped plate gives way to it.
    export --json The document and the whole catalog: what network-manager topology reads.
    export --json --cmdb The same as the CMDB would answer it (its objects and relations, with the catalog): the reference for reading the positions from the CMDB. Not used yet.
    report [--json] Everything the record holds, to the CMDB (Reports to the CMDB); --json prints the reports instead.

    mount add, mount modify, mount remove, mount list and mount show are the old names of place, place modify, unplace, place list and place show; device list, device show, device add, device modify, device remove and device record are the old names of the asset verbs; they answer for one release. A verb that names an asset takes its name or the role it plays. Placing an asset in stock makes it placed, unplacing it puts it back in stock; an ordered or disposed asset is not placed.

    Every changing verb takes --check (one line of what it would change — [check] would add room 'stue' at hareskov — or [refused] <why> and exit 1; --check --json prints the whole resulting document; the apply reports the same line, done), locks, checks the whole resulting document and writes it atomically; a change that would leave it invalid writes nothing. The verbs that name a device read the topology (network-manager topology --json --no-health --no-positions) unless --topology <file> hands one in; when it cannot be read, device names are not checked and a warning says so. The location checks read config/site.json. rack delete, room delete, asset dispose, unplace, cable remove, port remove and faceplate delete are destructive (the console asks for the name).

    A site whose config/racks.json the update has not renamed yet is read as it is; a change is refused until it has been. Likewise a positions.json that still records devices{}: each reads as an asset playing itself, and a change waits for migration 0027, which makes them assets and gives every node, switch and access point one of its own (srv-NN, sw-NN, ap-NN in the topology's order, the role's name as its display name).

    A box's life¶

    A server bought, racked, made a node, retired and sold (ADR-040 D7's example):

    asset-manager asset add --kind server --vendor HP --model Z840 --serial CZC1234ABC --stage ordered --supplier Proshop   # srv-07
asset-manager asset receive srv-07
asset-manager port add srv-07 ilo --kind rj45 --role bmc --mac aa:bb:cc:00:00:07
asset-manager place srv-07 --rack rack1 --u 12 --faceplate generic/server-2u
asset-manager asset allocate srv-07 --role tappaas5 --check     # once site-manager has added tappaas5
asset-manager asset allocate srv-07 --role tappaas5             # its placement is tappaas5's now
asset-manager asset fault srv-07 --note "fan 2 failed"          # operational and faulty
asset-manager asset repaired srv-07
asset-manager asset release srv-07                              # the node is gone; the box stays in its rack
asset-manager unplace srv-07
asset-manager asset dispose srv-07 --how sold --wiped

    A disk's life¶

    asset-manager disk adopt --check                    # the disks the servers' CIs know, once
asset-manager disk add srv-02 ata-HGST_HDN726050ALE614_NAHW317X --serial NAHW317X --kind hdd --size 5TB --bay 3
asset-manager disk list srv-02
asset-manager place dsk-04 --in srv-03              # moved to another server
asset-manager unplace dsk-04                        # pulled: in stock
asset-manager asset dispose dsk-04 --how recycled --wiped

    A machine with its disks: asset remove and asset dispose refuse one that holds disks until told what becomes of them. Each disk is its own step, a line of its own and its own report to the CMDB (a disk's CI ends before its asset's); the file is written once, and a refusal anywhere changes nothing:

    asset-manager asset remove srv-04 --with-disks --check
[check] would remove disk 'dsk-11' (serial S1), in srv-04, bay sda
[check] would remove disk 'dsk-12' (serial S2), in srv-04, bay sdb
[check] would remove asset 'srv-04'
asset-manager asset remove srv-04 --disks-to-stock             # the disks kept, in stock
asset-manager asset dispose srv-04 --how recycled --wiped --with-disks

    Recording a device¶

    A cable recorded before devices could be (the ISP's modem, named only on its cable) keeps working; validate points it out. Recording the device keeps the cable as it is:

    asset-manager asset add 'ISP modem' --kind modem --model generic/isp-modem --check
asset-manager asset add 'ISP modem' --kind modem --model generic/isp-modem
asset-manager place 'ISP modem' --room basement      # or --rack rack1 --u 3

    A device a switch port sees — a printer, a camera — is listed by the topology at its port with its MAC and what LLDP or its lease says. One verb records it and its cable:

    asset-manager asset record 'USW Pro XG 10 PoE:8' --name printer --kind other --check
asset-manager asset record aa:bb:cc:00:11:22 --name printer --kind other
asset-manager place printer --room office

    Reports to the CMDB¶

    asset-manager is a CMDB source (ADR-040 D4; "cmdbSource": true in components.json). config/positions.json is still the record; the CMDB is told what it holds, as source: asset-manager, every object and relation marked recorded: true:

    positions.json CMDB
    a room room, at its location (by name; site-manager reports the location)
    a rack rack, placed-in its room (room field); width in inches
    an asset its D3 class: server; network-device (switch, access-point); appliance (modem, ont, ups); passive-device (patch-panel, pdu); other-device — with deviceType, vendor, model, serial, assetTag, stage (as derived: placed, operational), faulty, faultNote, procurement, disposal
    its ports (its own, merged over its model's plate, else the plate it is placed with) port, part-of the asset; kind, speed, standard, bands, module, role, mac, side
    a disk in an asset disk, part-of that asset (its key with the tag, and immutable: a moved disk's CI in the old asset ends and one in the new begins); bay, vendor, model, serial, wwn, size, kind, bus, firmware, assetTag, the by-id name shown, stage, faulty, faultNote, procurement, disposal. A disk in stock has no CI; site-manager adds what the node says of it (health, holds, partitions, and SMART from the sweep)
    a placement placed-in from the asset to its rack (u, height, width, slot, face, faceplate, label, description on the relation) or its room
    a recorded cable connects port to port (kind, label, origin, side, linkSpeed)
    an asset's role allocated-to the node of that name ({class: node, name}: the machine, vm or device CI another manager reports) 
    asset-manager report               # all of it: one batch, and one report per allocation
asset-manager report --json        # the reports, not sent
asset-manager export --json --cmdb # the same, as the CMDB would answer a query for it
    • Every verb that writes positions.json (the asset, port, place, cable, room and rack verbs) reports what it changed, after writing it: a change with the verb, the console's job id (TAPPAAS_JOB_ID), the actor (TAPPAAS_ACTOR, else the OS user), the objects and relations that differ and what they name; what it removed is ended (end: true). A faceplate verb changes nothing the CMDB holds and reports nothing. --check reports nothing.
    • Never a gate. A report cmdb-controller cannot take now is spooled under config/.cmdb-spool/ and replayed by the next report that reaches it (or cmdb rescan); one it refuses is a warning. The verb is done either way.
    • Allocations go apart, one report each: the controller refuses a whole batch when a reference does not resolve, and the node an asset plays may not be in the CMDB yet. A refused allocation is a warning that names the node; the next asset-manager report sends it again.
    • Not reported (printed by report, listed under skipped by --json): a room with no location while site.json has no primary one; a rack in no recorded room (its placements with it); a placement no asset is recorded for; a cable with an end that is no recorded asset's port (a node's NIC on a generic plate). An asset's own location (a location of site.json, with no room) has no relation in the metamodel yet.

    The faceplate catalog¶

    faceplates/<vendor>/<model>.json, one plate each:

    {
  "vendor": "UniFi", "model": "USW Pro XG 10 PoE", "match": ["USWED77"],
  "kind": "switch", "units": 1, "width": "full",
  "ports": "10xpoe:Port {n}+2xsfp+:Port {n}[51-98]",
  "labels": [{ "text": "UniFi", "at": "left" }]
}

    or, a grid right of the plate's name:

    {
  "vendor": "UniFi", "model": "Nano HD", "kind": "ap", "units": 1, "width": "third",
  "rows": [[{ "name": "Port 1", "id": "uplink", "kind": "poe" }]]
}
    • rows are the port rows top to bottom (null is a gap), drawn right of the plate's name. A plate whose ports have a layout gives ports, a port spec, instead (below): its ports in groups, each over its share of the plate's width. A port's name is the controller's ("Port 11", "sfp-sfpplus1"); its id, the topology's, is the number of "Port N", else the name, unless given. kind is how it is drawn — rj45, sfp+, sfp28, poe, console or power — and says the asset's port: rj45 and poe an rj45 port at 1g, sfp+ an sfp cage at 10g, sfp28 one at 25g; a console and a power inlet are drawn and are no ports. speed gives another (a 10G RJ45: "speed": "10g"), one its connector comes in.
    • match lists the models and names the inventories report the device by: the catalog's suggestion for a device is the plate whose model or match is its model or name.
    • generic nics fills the plate from a node's NICs (its uplinks), ports from a switch's ports (odd over even). The generic plates are the suggestion for a device the catalog lacks: a node gets generic/server-2u, a switch generic/switch-1u.
    • passive marks a device nothing can see (patch panel, PDU, modem).

    A new model is a new file; faceplates show <key> checks it without the console.

    The port spec¶

    How faceplate add --ports and a catalog file's ports write a plate's ports:

    • groups stand side by side, a comma between them and a gap between them on the plate;
    • rows within a group, top to bottom, a slash between them;
    • items within a row, a plus between them: [<n>x]<kind>[-<speed>][@<rows>][:<name>], a kind rj45, sfp+, sfp28, poe, console, power, or gap (an empty place). A speed (1g, 2.5g, 10g, 25g) is the port's maximum where it is not its kind's — rj45-2.5g, poe-10g, sfp+-1g — and one its connector comes in (rj45-25g is refused). A kind ending in + takes its own + before the next item: 2xsfp++rj45.

    Ports are numbered 1, 2, … unless named; {n} in a name is the number that name counts from 1, and a name without it given to several ports gets the number after it. <n>x<kind>@<k>, alone in its group, lays its ports in k rows, odd over even, as stacked cages are. A group may end in its place on the plate, [<from>-<to>] in percent of its width — every group, or none: then they stand right of the plate's name, packed to its right edge.

    Spec The plate
    24xrj45 24 ports in one row, 1-24
    8xrj45, 2xsfp+ eight RJ45, a gap, two SFP+ (9, 10)
    8xsfp+/8xsfp+ two rows of eight, 1-8 over 9-16
    16xsfp+@2:sfp-sfpplus{n}[3-66] sixteen cages odd over even, in the left two thirds
    rj45:WAN+gap+4xrj45:LAN {n} WAN, a gap, LAN 1-4
    10xpoe-10g:Port {n}+2xsfp+:Port {n}[51-98] ten 10G PoE RJ45 and two SFP+ cages, over the right half

    The site's own plates¶

    A site adds a plate for a model the catalog lacks; it is kept in config/positions.json under faceplates, keyed <vendor>/<model>, and merged over the shipped catalog, so a placement, an asset's model and a cable end name it as they name a shipped one:

    asset-manager faceplate add acme/nas-4 --units 2 --ports "4xrj45, 2xsfp+" --kind server --match NAS-4X --check
asset-manager faceplate add acme/nas-4 --units 2 --ports "4xrj45, 2xsfp+" --kind server --match NAS-4X
asset-manager place nas --rack rack1 --u 6 --faceplate acme/nas-4

    It is checked as a shipped plate is: a broken one is an error and is not drawn. A site plate of a shipped key replaces the shipped plate for the site, and validate says so; deleting it brings the shipped plate back. The generic plates stay the fallback for a model neither has. To contribute a plate upstream, faceplates show <key> --file prints it as faceplates/<vendor>/<model>.json.

    Lifecycle¶

    install.sh / update.sh build the flake package and link asset-manager (and position-manager and rack-manager, its old names, for one release) into ~/bin; the package carries the catalog beside the compiled sources. test.sh is offline: the unit suite over hrossen's topology and the CLI against a temp config, its CMDB client pointed at a socket nobody serves (the reports land in the temp config's spool, where the suite reads them). test/unit/cmdb.test.ts checks report over hrossen's positions.json as migration 0027 leaves it against cmdb-controller's metamodel/*.json; what the controller does with it is cmdb-controller's test. validate.sh runs asset-manager validate.
